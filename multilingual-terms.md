# ADDIS Methodology — Decision Thinking & Policy Co-Creation

## Origin

ADDIS was developed by the Innovation for Policy Foundation (i4Policy) through six years
of work with governments and communities in 80+ countries. It is a mnemonic for the five
phases of a participatory decision-making process, broken down into 12 concrete steps.

Sources:
- Overview: [decisionthinking.org/the-addis-methodology](https://decisionthinking.org/the-addis-methodology/)
- Canonical template: **Decision Thinking Template (ADDIS v5.3)**, Innovation for Policy
  Foundation, 2024 — CC BY-SA 4.0, contact community@i4policy.org

**Why it matters for foresight practice:** Foresight produces futures — possible, plausible,
probable, preferable. ADDIS is what turns a preferred future into an **adopted, implemented,
and monitored decision**. Foresight without a decision-thinking process risks staying at the
"interesting report" stage. ADDIS without foresight risks optimizing decisions for the wrong
future. Used together, they form a full pipeline: **explore futures → decide → act → learn**.

**Naming note:** the website overview names the Drafting phase's third step "Consult." The
canonical downloadable template (v5.3) names it **"Review"** — same definition ("Share drafts
for inputs and then integrate feedback"). This reference uses **Review** as the primary term,
matching the official template, with "Consult" noted as an equivalent.

## Template Header Fields

Before working through the 12 steps, the official template opens with four framing fields.
Fill these first — they anchor every subsequent step.

| Field | Guiding Question |
|---|---|
| **Title** | Name of the decision process |
| **Policy Vision** | What is the long-term outcome you hope to achieve? |
| **Policy Goal** | What are the concrete goals or objectives this policy is trying to achieve? |
| **Who is Concerned?** | Who are the people, groups, communities, or stakeholders concerned by the decision? |
| **Decision Instrument** | What is the most appropriate type of instrument — contract, law, policy, strategy, diagram, etc.? |

**Decision Instrument** is the field foresight practitioners most often skip — it forces an
early answer to "what form will this decision actually take?" (a law is not drafted the same
way as a strategy or a diagram).

## The 5 Phases and 12 Steps

### Phase 1 — Agenda Setting
*A topic is identified and explored; specific issues and opportunities are defined and
prioritized to come up with a plan.*

| Step | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Care** | Understand the situation by empathizing with the people and organizations affected |
| **Initiate** | Launch a process with relevant collaborators and a clear vision |
| **Define** | Identify and prioritize the specific issues or conditions to be addressed |

### Phase 2 — Drafting
*Potential solutions are explored and developed into concrete proposals and decision
instruments (contracts, laws, policies, strategies, visions, constitutions, diagrams, etc.),
and refined through feedback.*

| Step | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Ideate** | Brainstorm, benchmark, and evaluate potential solutions |
| **Develop** | Transform potential solutions into drafts |
| **Review** *(aka Consult)* | Share drafts for inputs and then integrate feedback |

### Phase 3 — Decision-Making
*Decisions are proposed and then taken through relevant norms and processes.*

| Step | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Propose** | Suggest a decision |
| **Adopt** | Approve a decision through appropriate processes |

### Phase 4 — Implementation
*The phase in which decisions are put into practice and monitored.*

| Step | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Deliver** | Communicate and execute the agreed decision |
| **Monitor** | Track and assess implementation of a decision against its desired results |

### Phase 5 — Sense-Making
*Learnings are gathered, organized, and understood to improve future decisions.*

| Step | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Harvest** | Gather insights from implementation and the overall process |
| **Understand** | Make sense of observations to inform and inspire future decisions |

**ADDIS is a loop, not a line.** Understand feeds back into Care — sense-making from one
decision cycle becomes the starting point for agenda-setting on the next.

## Integrating ADDIS with Foresight — Method Bridge

Foresight methods and ADDIS steps map onto each other. Use this table to sequence a combined
foresight + decision-thinking engagement.

| ADDIS Phase | ADDIS Step | Foresight Method That Feeds It |
|---|---|---|
| Agenda Setting | Care | Expert panel design, stakeholder mapping (MACTOR) |
| Agenda Setting | Initiate | Phase 0 scoping (client brief), Study Design Document |
| Agenda Setting | Define | STEEP scan, signal classification grid, MICMAC variable selection |
| Drafting | Ideate | Scenario construction (Shell/Schwartz axes), Futures Wheel, CLA reconstruction |
| Drafting | Develop | Backcasting — turning a preferred scenario into a draft roadmap |
| Drafting | Review | Scenario validation workshop (see consulting-program.md Phase 3) |
| Decision-Making | Propose | Wind Tunneling output — the strategy that survives across scenarios becomes the proposal |
| Decision-Making | Adopt | Steering committee sign-off (see consulting-program.md governance) |
| Implementation | Deliver | Communication deliverable stack, executive presentation |
| Implementation | Monitor | Early Warning System — indicators tied to each scenario |
| Sense-Making | Harvest | Post-implementation review against original scenarios |
| Sense-Making | Understand | Updated environmental scan — feeds the next foresight cycle |

**Practical rule:** When a foresight engagement must end in an *adopted policy or institutional
decision* (not just a report), overlay ADDIS on top of the 5-phase consulting program
(`consulting-program.md`). The consulting phases produce the analytical content; ADDIS
provides the governance and legitimacy process that gets it adopted and acted on.

---

## When to Use ADDIS-Overlaid Foresight

| Situation | Use ADDIS overlay? |
|---|---|
| Public policy foresight (government, ministry, public agency) | **Yes — strongly recommended** |
| Multi-stakeholder decisions requiring buy-in (federation, consortium, NGO coalition) | **Yes** |
| Citizen or community engagement components | **Yes — ADDIS is participatory by design** |
| Internal corporate strategy with a single decision-maker | Optional — Wind Tunneling + Backcasting alone may suffice |
| Pure exploratory/academic foresight with no decision attached | **No** — ADDIS assumes a decision is the goal |

## Participatory Design Principles (from ADDIS practice)

ADDIS was built around **citizen and stakeholder co-creation**, not top-down expert delivery.
When applying it, hold to these principles:

1. **Care before Define** — do not jump to defining the problem before genuinely understanding
   who is affected and how. Skipping Care produces solutions that miss lived reality.
2. **Review is not a formality** — feedback from the Review step must visibly change the
   draft. A consultation that doesn't alter anything erodes trust for future cycles.
3. **Adopt requires a legitimate process** — not just sign-off by the most senior person in the
   room. Legitimacy varies by context (a vote, a public comment period, a council decision).
4. **Monitor must be pre-committed** — the indicators to track should be defined during
   Propose/Adopt, not invented after Deliver when it's convenient to declare success.
5. **Harvest is mandatory, not optional** — many foresight/policy engagements stop at
   Implementation. The Sense-Making phase is what prevents the same mistakes recurring in
   the next cycle. Build it into the contract/mandate from the start.

## ADDIS Canvas — Official Template Layout

Reproduces the structure of the canonical Decision Thinking Template (ADDIS v5.3). Fill the
4 header fields first, then work through the 12 steps left to right, phase by phase.

**Header:**

```
Title: ____________________________________________________________

Policy Vision          Policy Goal            Who is Concerned?      Decision Instrument
(long-term outcome)    (concrete objectives)   (stakeholders)         (contract/law/policy/
                                                                       strategy/diagram/etc.)
```

**12-step body — one guiding question per cell, answered with stakeholders:**

| Phase | Step | Guiding Question |
|---|---|---|
| Agenda Setting | Care | Who is affected by this decision? How do they experience the issue today? |
| Agenda Setting | Initiate | Who needs to be at the table? What's the mandate and timeline? |
| Agenda Setting | Define | What are the 3–5 priority issues? What's explicitly out of scope? |
| Drafting | Ideate | What futures/options exist? *(link to scenario construction)* |
| Drafting | Develop | What is the draft decision/roadmap? *(link to backcasting)* |
| Drafting | Review | Who reviewed the draft? What changed as a result? |
| Decision-Making | Propose | What is the final proposed decision? |
| Decision-Making | Adopt | Who approved it, through what process, and when? |
| Implementation | Deliver | How and when is this communicated and executed? |
| Implementation | Monitor | What indicators track success? Who owns tracking them? *(link to Early Warning System)* |
| Sense-Making | Harvest | What did we learn? What worked, what didn't? |
| Sense-Making | Understand | What does this mean for the next cycle? |
